What We Measure

Systemic risk is the probability that stress in one system propagates into cascading failure across interconnected systems. We track 50 indicators across 8 domains chosen for historical coupling to prior crises, data availability, and forward-looking signal.

The differentiating contribution is tracking coupling — the degree to which stress in one domain is currently interacting with another. That interaction is what historically separates contained crises from systemic collapses.

Indicator Normalization

Each raw indicator is normalized to 0–100 against its 10-year historical distribution: 100 = worst ever recorded, 0 = best ever. Indicators tagged lead (predictive, 4–12 week horizon) receive a 20% weight premium within their domain score. Indicators tagged CB are circuit breakers that override the composite to ≥ 92 if triggered.

Domain Score Formula
D_score = Σ (indicator × weight)
          ÷ Σ weights

Leading indicators: weight × 1.20
Circuit breakers: override rule applies
Composite & Coupling Multiplier
Base = Σ (D_score × D_weight) ÷ Σ D_weights
C = count(domains where D_score ≥ 60)
M = 1 + max(0, C−1) × 0.06
Composite = min(100, Base × M)

The multiplier M penalizes simultaneous multi-domain stress. Two domains at 70 is materially more dangerous than one at 100 and one at 40.

Circuit Breaker Override

If any circuit breaker fires (DEFCON ≤ 3, WHO pandemic phase ≥ 5, 3+ major exchange halts, nuclear emergency), the composite is overridden to minimum 92 regardless of domain scores. The model stops mattering when the event is the signal.
